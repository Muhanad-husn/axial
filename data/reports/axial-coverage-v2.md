## What this measures

The main report describes what Axial does and how well it answered a set of test
questions. This companion answers a different question: **what is actually in the
library, and where is it thin?**

Axial reads the thirty-five books passage by passage and builds a page for every
name any passage mentions — every scholar, every country, every institution,
every concept. The index that results holds **47,584 name pages**, built from
**6,842 passages** across **35 books**. This document counts how those passages
spread across those pages, by kind of name.

Two numbers describe each page. Its **passage count** is how many passages
mention that name. Its **book count** is how many of the thirty-five books those
passages come from. The first says how much the library has to say about a name;
the second says whether more than one author is saying it. A page with fifty
passages from one book is a summary. A page with fifty passages from eight books
is an argument.

Everything below is counted mechanically from the finished index. No model was
asked to judge anything.

*"Mentions" counts page-and-passage pairs, so a passage that names three concepts
is counted three times. A further 85 pages carry a kind label outside these eight
and are omitted from the rows above; they are included in every total.*

The distribution is the same shape in every kind and it is severe. **The median
page of every kind holds exactly one passage.** Across the whole index, 32,447 of
the 47,584 pages — roughly two in three — are mentioned once and never again.
Another quarter are mentioned two to four times. Fewer than six hundred pages in
the entire library are mentioned thirty times or more.

The book counts are the sharper finding. **83.0% of all pages exist inside a
single book.** They are that author's vocabulary, that author's cast of
characters, that author's examples — recorded faithfully, but never met by anyone
else on the shelf.

Two kinds stand out against the pattern. Countries and places hold 35,493
mentions off only 4,670 pages, which is more than twice the density of any other
kind, and they are the least likely to be trapped in one book. Works — the books
and papers the authors cite — are the opposite: 94.2% appear in a single book,
which is what a bibliography looks like.

## What a page needs before it can carry a comparison

A page is useful for research when two things are true at once: enough passages
to have something to compare, and enough different books for the comparison to be
between authors rather than within one. In practice that means roughly **thirty
to two hundred passages drawn from five or more books** — below thirty there is
too little, above two hundred no reader can hold it, and under five books it is
one author talking.

**329 of the 47,584 pages meet that bar.** Seven in ten thousand.

| Kind of name | Pages | Meet the bar | Share of kind |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Country or place | 4,670 | 129 | 2.76% |
| Movement or religion | 1,247 | 28 | 2.25% |
| Historical period | 1,219 | 10 | 0.82% |
| Event | 2,915 | 20 | 0.69% |
| Institution or group | 9,921 | 56 | 0.56% |
| Person | 12,770 | 64 | 0.50% |
| Concept | 7,953 | 18 | 0.23% |
| Work | 6,809 | 4 | 0.06% |

Places are still the library's strongest entry points by a wide margin — 129 of
the 329, two in five. That is a real property of this shelf, not an accident of
counting: it is a corpus organised around cases, and its authors meet each other
over Syria, Iraq, Britain and France far more often than over any idea.

The bar now bites at the top as well as the bottom. Six pages that qualified at
thirty-one books — *West*, *UN*, *Jordan*, *Paris*, *Aleppo* and *Ba'th Party* —
have passed two hundred passages and left it. They did not get worse; they got
too big to read in one sitting. At this rate the ceiling, not the floor, becomes
the binding constraint on the library's best pages.

## Concepts are the thinnest kind

Concepts are what a reader would expect to be the backbone of a library about
state formation and nationalism. They are still the weakest kind in it, and four
new books did not change that.

There are 7,953 concept pages. **5,930 of them — three in four — are mentioned
exactly once in thirty-five books.** Only 171 concepts reach ten mentions, only
22 reach thirty, and only **135 are discussed by five or more books at all**.
Eighteen meet the research bar above.

## What this says about which book to add next

Version 1.0 named four additions. The four books that arrived instead were three
Syria case studies and one theory of conflict, so **three of the four
recommendations are untouched and still stand.**

**1. A second voice on sovereignty. Still the cheapest single change available.**
Jackson 1990 supplies 87% of *negative sovereignty*, 81% of *positive
sovereignty*, 61% of *quasi-states* and 49% of *self-determination*. Every one of
those figures is unchanged from version 1.0. Only two other books on the shelf
touch the vocabulary at all. One more sovereignty theorist would turn a private
vocabulary into a contested one.

**2. A critic of Michael Mann, not a fifth Mann. Untouched.** Mann's four volumes
contribute about 31,000 links between them, and the library's largest concept
pages are largely internal to that body of work — *IEMP model* is 82% one
commentary on Mann, *neoliberalism* 73% Mann's own fourth volume.

**3. Something that answers Chouliaraki and Agamben. Untouched.** Chouliaraki
2024 still supplies 100% of *Black Lives Matter* and *Me Too* and holds 136
concepts alone. Agamben 2005 still supplies 93% of *state of exception* on the
smallest footprint of any book and holds 188 concepts alone.

**4. The Syria case is now the library's deep end, and that changes what to add
next.** Gelvin, Hinnebusch and Wedeen put 12,468 links onto the shelf and pushed
twenty-nine pages into the research band, nearly all of them Syrian or late-
Ottoman. *Ba'th Party* went from 116 passages to 288 and *Aleppo* from 166 to
275 — both now past the readable ceiling. The next Syria book adds less than the
last three did. What the deep end now lacks is a book that argues *with* it: this
shelf has four accounts of Syrian authoritarianism and no sustained
counter-argument to any of them.

Behind all four is one governing fact, unchanged. **Concept pages in this library
are 85.9% single-book.** A book on a new topic makes the pile of once-mentioned
names larger — the four new books created 3,710 pages nothing else on the shelf
touches. A book that re-argues the concepts already carrying ten or more passages
is what moves pages into the range where the engine can compare authors at all.

## How these numbers were counted

Every figure comes from `scratchpad/coverage_counts.py`, run against
`data/vault/notes.db` on 6 August 2026. A page's kind is taken from the index's
`names` table. A page's passage count is its distinct passages, and its book
count its distinct sources.

**Back matter is included, as it was in version 1.0.** Since #661, bibliographies
and indexes are no longer retrievable as evidence, and counting the index without
them gives a smaller library: 42,026 pages from 6,319 passages, of which 315 meet
the research bar. The proportions are the same in both views; the raw counts are
kept here so the two versions of this report can be compared.
