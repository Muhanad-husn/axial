## What this measures

The main report describes what Axial does and how well it answered a set of test questions. This companion answers a different question: **what is actually in the library, and where is it thin?**

Axial reads the thirty-one books passage by passage and builds a page for every name any passage mentions — every scholar, every country, every institution, every concept. The index that results holds **49,674 name pages**, built from **6,092 passages** across **31 books**. This document counts how those passages spread across those pages, by kind of name.

Two numbers describe each page. Its **passage count** is how many passages mention that name. Its **book count** is how many of the thirty-one books those passages come from. The first says how much the library has to say about a name; the second says whether more than one author is saying it. A page with fifty passages from one book is a summary. A page with fifty passages from eight books is an argument.

Everything below is counted mechanically from the finished index. No model was asked to judge anything.

| Concept | 7,614 | 5,673 | 1,449 | 320 | 149 | 20 | 3 | 14,756 | 86.0% |
| Country or place | 4,800 | 2,818 | 1,180 | 352 | 262 | 132 | 56 | 35,575 | 73.5% |
| Event | 2,818 | 2,009 | 605 | 128 | 54 | 17 | 5 | 7,393 | 83.9% |
| Movement or religion | 1,263 | 785 | 297 | 83 | 69 | 26 | 3 | 5,269 | 74.3% |
| Historical period | 1,181 | 835 | 251 | 55 | 29 | 9 | 2 | 3,087 | 77.6% |

*"Mentions" counts page-and-passage pairs, so a passage that names three concepts is counted three times.*

The distribution is the same shape in every kind and it is severe. **The median page of every kind holds exactly one passage.** Across the whole index, 32,640 of the 49,674 pages — roughly two in three — are mentioned once and never again. Another quarter are mentioned two to four times. Fewer than five hundred pages in the entire library are mentioned thirty times or more.

The book counts are the sharper finding. **About 84% of all pages exist inside a single book.** They are that author's vocabulary, that author's cast of characters, that author's examples — recorded faithfully, but never met by anyone else on the shelf.

Two kinds stand out against the pattern. Countries and places hold 35,575 mentions off only 4,800 pages, which is more than twice the density of any other kind, and they are the least likely to be trapped in one book. Works — the books and papers the authors cite — are the opposite: 94.6% appear in a single book, which is what a bibliography looks like.

## What a page needs before it can carry a comparison

A page is useful for research when two things are true at once: enough passages to have something to compare, and enough different books for the comparison to be between authors rather than within one. In practice that means roughly **thirty to two hundred passages drawn from five or more books** — below thirty there is too little, above two hundred no reader can hold it, and under five books it is one author talking.

**329 of the 49,674 pages meet that bar.** Seven in ten thousand.

| Kind of name | Pages | Meet the bar | Share of kind |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Country or place | 4,800 | 143 | 2.98% |
| Movement or religion | 1,263 | 24 | 1.90% |
| Historical period | 1,181 | 9 | 0.76% |
| Event | 2,818 | 18 | 0.64% |
| Institution or group | 9,898 | 56 | 0.57% |
| Person | 13,416 | 55 | 0.41% |
| Concept | 7,614 | 19 | 0.25% |
| Work | 8,583 | 5 | 0.06% |

Places are the library's strongest entry points by a wide margin — 143 of the 329, nearly half. That is a real property of this shelf, not an accident of counting: it is a corpus organised around cases, and its authors meet each other over Syria, Iraq, Britain and France far more often than over any idea.

## Concepts are the thinnest kind

Concepts are what a reader would expect to be the backbone of a library about state formation and nationalism. They are the weakest kind in it.

There are 7,614 concept pages. **5,673 of them — three in four — are mentioned exactly once in thirty-one books.** Only 172 concepts reach ten mentions, only 23 reach thirty, and only **126 are discussed by five or more books at all**. Nineteen meet the research bar above.

## What this says about which book to add next

The counts above point at four specific additions, in rough order of how much each would change the library.

**1. A second voice on sovereignty.** Jackson 1990 supplies 87% of *negative sovereignty*, 81% of *positive sovereignty*, 61% of *quasi-states* and 50% of *self-determination*. Only two other books on the shelf touch the vocabulary at all. One more sovereignty theorist would turn a private vocabulary into a contested one, and it is the cheapest single change available.

**2. A critic of Michael Mann, not a fifth Mann.** Mann's four volumes contribute about 31,000 links between them, and the library's largest concept pages are largely internal to that body of work — *IEMP model* is 82% one commentary on Mann, *neoliberalism* 77% Mann's own fourth volume. Another Mann volume would add volume. A book arguing against him would add argument.

**3. Something that answers Chouliaraki and Agamben.** Chouliaraki 2024 supplies 100% of *Black Lives Matter*, *Me Too* and *emotional capitalism*, and holds 147 concepts alone. Agamben 2005 supplies 93% of *state of exception* on the smallest footprint of any book, and holds 187 concepts alone. Both are dense and neither is answered by anything else on the shelf.

**4. Check the two thin entries before adding anything.** Kandiah 2018 and Malešević 2026 barely register in the index. Either the text did not come through cleanly or they do not belong on this shelf; that is worth establishing before the shelf grows.

Behind all four is one governing fact. **Concept pages in this library are 86% single-book.** A book on a new topic makes the pile of once-mentioned names larger. A book that re-argues the concepts already carrying ten or more passages is what moves pages into the range where the engine can compare authors at all.
